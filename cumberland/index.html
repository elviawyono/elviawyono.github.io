Cumberland Project

Using Scenario-Based Visualization to Support Community Engagement in District Energy Planning

Project Overview

This visualization work is part of the broader ACET Cumberland District Energy Project, which explores the potential for using warm water from abandoned, flooded coal mines to heat and cool buildings in the Village of Cumberland, British Columbia.

The project investigates how minewater geoexchange could support cleaner and more resilient community development by reducing emissions, lowering energy costs, and supporting future developments such as affordable housing, public facilities, and local economic activity.

The proposed pilot focuses on a minewater geoexchange system that exchanges energy with water in the abandoned coal mines beneath the village. Using heat pumps and closed-loop circulation, this system could provide low-carbon heating and cooling for buildings in the municipal downtown precinct and nearby eco-industrial park.

My work focuses on using scenario-based landscape visualization to help communicate this complex energy system in a more accessible and place-based way. By visualizing possible future scenarios, the project aims to support community engagement, dialogue, and shared understanding around what clean energy transitions could look like in place.

Approach

The project combines landscape visualization, scenario development, and community-oriented communication approaches to help participants explore possible future energy systems and their impacts on place.

Visualization scenarios were developed using 3D spatial modeling and environmental rendering workflows to create immersive and understandable representations of possible energy futures.

Why Visualization Matters

Clean energy transitions are not only technical challenges, they are also social and spatial questions that require communication, participation, and place-based understanding.

Scenario-based visualization can help communities:

  • See possible future conditions
  • Understand spatial implications
  • Support dialogue and reflection
  • Engage with planning discussions more meaningfully
