PhD research in progress

Collaborative Land-Use Futures in Fort Saskatchewan

Exploring how systems mapping and visualization can support planning conversations.

Project Context

Understanding land-use connections through systems mapping

This project explores how participatory systems mapping and landscape visualization can support collaborative land-use planning in Fort Saskatchewan, Alberta. Using a climate–biodiversity–health lens, the research looks at how land-use decisions may connect to community well-being, recreation, environmental conditions, and long-term planning priorities.

Why Fort Saskatchewan?

A place-based planning context

Fort Saskatchewan brings together urban growth, parks and recreation, river valley landscapes, industrial activity, agricultural land, and community infrastructure. These overlapping systems make it a useful context for exploring relationships, trade-offs, and possible future directions.
